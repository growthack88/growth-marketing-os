# MENA notes for experiments

## Commitment and payment

- **Cash on delivery is not commitment.** A COD order is a "maybe" until it's delivered. Confirmation and delivery rates decide whether it turns into revenue. For validation, take money that has actually moved: a refundable deposit or a prepaid founding price.
- **Offer the payment rails people use.** Card-only checkouts exclude many buyers. In Egypt: instant bank transfer (e.g. InstaPay), mobile wallets, and payment links from local gateways. In the Gulf: cards, Apple Pay, and local schemes (e.g. mada in KSA). Confirm current options with the user's payment provider. Don't assume.
- **If a pre-sale underperforms, check payment friction before concluding "no demand".** Call people who started checkout and didn't pay.
- **Refund promises must be real** and easy to claim (WhatsApp is often the expected support channel).

## Channels for reaching strangers

- Paid social (Meta, TikTok, and Snapchat in KSA), search, and Click-to-WhatsApp ads.
- Communities: niche Facebook groups, WhatsApp communities and channels, Telegram, LinkedIn for B2B. Follow each group's rules, and ask admins before posting.
- WhatsApp works for interviews, waitlist follow-ups and pre-sale reminders, with opt-in only.

## Language

- Write test assets in the target market's register: Egyptian Arabic for Egypt, Gulf dialect for KSA/UAE, MSA when the audience is mixed or formal. Keep product and marketing terms in English where that's how people talk.
- Test dialect vs. MSA as its own experiment (card A5). Don't mix countries in one test.

## Timing

- **Ramadan** changes daily rhythms: activity shifts to late evening and after iftar, and purchase intent changes by category. Don't compare a Ramadan test with a non-Ramadan baseline.
- **Weekends differ by country** (Friday–Saturday in Egypt and KSA, Saturday–Sunday in the UAE since 2022). Run tests in **full weeks** so weekday effects even out, and don't merge countries with different weekends into one daily readout.
- Big sales periods (White Friday, Eid, national days) distort costs and intent. Avoid them for baseline tests, or test only inside them.

## Costs

- There are **no reliable public MENA CPC benchmarks**. Measure CPC with a small pilot before setting pass lines.
- Media costs differ by country by large multiples (see benchmarks-used). Split budgets and readouts by country.

## Privacy and consent

- Collect only what the test needs. Say what you'll use contact details for. Get opt-in before WhatsApp follow-ups. Local data-protection laws apply (e.g. KSA PDPL, Egypt's data-protection law, UAE PDPL). Point users to them; don't give legal advice.
